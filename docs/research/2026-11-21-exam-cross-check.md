# TE1 against the matriculation exam: cross-check report, brief 003

2026-11-21. Four readers, one per year, and a critic. Eight sessions (spring and autumn 2023–2026) and 72 tasks
were mapped, with 1,680 points offered (210 per session; a candidate answers five tasks for 120). The raw mapping, task
by task with YTL links, is in `2026-11-21-exam-cross-check.json`. Tasks are described in our own words, never copied.
Grades: [A] read on YTL's grading pages (the critic spot-checked four sessions); [M] the critic's reading.

## In plain words

1. **TE1 is the main subject of about a quarter of the exam, and present in more than half.**
   - TE1 is the main subject of 28% of points [A].
   - It is mixed with TE2 or TE3 in another 31%; TE2-only tasks are 21%, TE3-only 21% [A].
   - The TE1 share swings from 19% to 50% between sessions.
   - A student with only TE1 cannot cover the exam. TE1-primary tasks also expect alcohol and nicotine as lifestyle
     factors, which TE1 does not treat.
2. **Our ten topics cover the exam's TE1 content partly, rarely fully.**
   - Of the points that draw on TE1: full coverage 13%, partial 81%, none 6% [M].
   - The topics have the right subjects, but not the specific concepts and facts the exam asks for.
3. **The TE1 areas asked most** are nutrition, exercise, rest and sleep (26 task parts), the determinants of health
   and health promotion (21), and mental health and stress (14). The behaviour models, health culture and sexual
   health are occasional (5 each). Study wellbeing appeared once in eight sessions [A].
4. **The single most valuable addition** is the WHO/Ottawa five action areas of health promotion. They were the frame
   for proposals in four of the eight sessions (about 92 points), and no TE1 topic names them.
5. **The task types differ.**
   - Our 30 exam tasks are almost all built on fictional text, and six of the ten 30-point tasks share one template.
   - The real exam uses charts, maps, statistics tables, a sleep hypnogram, product labels, images and video in
     about a quarter of tasks.
   - It also uses formats we lack: list-and-classify with a count rubric, naming concepts, multiple choice with
     penalty, define-and-exemplify, drawing a model or chart, applying a given framework, and critiquing a concept.
   - Half of each 20- and 30-point task is scored on information processing, with deductions when the given
     material is not used.

## What TE2 and TE3 must carry [M]

- **TE2:**
  - research designs and the reliability of a study report; survey versus register data;
  - cognitive biases and the concept of risk;
  - health communication, advertising, social media and AI-produced health text;
  - alcohol, nicotine and drugs, dependence and the brain, with chart tasks;
  - the physical environment (radon, indoor air, noise, water), sustainable development, work ability.
- **TE3:**
  - the epidemiological transition, infectious diseases and vaccines, risk factors for chronic diseases and cancer;
  - explanation models of disease and public-health history;
  - health inequality with figure reading, and Health in All Policies;
  - the ethical-analysis perspectives applied to prioritisation, biobanks, abortion and communicable-disease law;
  - self-care and services for families.
