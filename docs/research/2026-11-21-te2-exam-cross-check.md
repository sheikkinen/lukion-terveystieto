# TE2 against the matriculation exam: cross-check report, brief 008

2026-11-21. A re-mapper and a critic, reusing brief 003's mapping of all 72 tasks of the eight exams from 2023 to
2026. The raw results are in `2026-11-21-te2-exam-cross-check.json`. Grades: [A] on YTL's grading pages (the critic
spot-checked four tasks); [M] the agents' judgement.

## In plain words

1. **TE2 content is in about 28–31% of exam points** [M], not the 35–45% I expected. TE2-only tasks are about 21%;
   the rest are TE2 strands inside mixed tasks.
2. **Our TE2 pages cover that content well:** about 55% fully, 43% partly and 2–3% not at all [M]. That's much better
   than TE1 before its exam drive (13% fully). The reasoning-heavy topics carry it: biases, health communication,
   survey versus register data, the Ottawa areas, the mechanisms of dependence.
3. **The gaps are specific facts, which cost content points:**
   - **research vocabulary:** empirical, qualitative versus quantitative, mixed methods, cohort, retrospective, and
     significance in words;
   - **risk concepts:** relative and absolute risk, exposure, and subjective risk;
   - **radon:** lung cancer, the much higher risk for smokers, and remediation;
   - **indoor air and water safety;**
   - **substance-specific harms, in words only:** alcohol (liver, cancers, the fetus, FASD), tobacco (COPD, lung
     cancer), e-cigarettes, nicotine pouches, cannabis and psychosis;
   - **alcohol policy levers:** price and tax, the Alko monopoly, opening hours, marketing bans;
   - **working-life change since 1950,** and the work-ability house;
   - **smaller items:** Dunning-Kruger and other biases, placebo, climate anxiety as a concept, loneliness and FOMO,
     and packaging claims.
4. **Task formats mostly match** the real exam: multiple choice with penalty, naming concepts, finding errors,
   completing tables, applying the Ottawa areas, and a research plan. **Missing:**
   - a 20-item true/false set;
   - list-and-count tasks;
   - map, label and video material described in text;
   - two-series charts;
   - stated character limits.
5. **TE3 must carry:**
   - disease epidemiology (cancer risk factors, COPD, incidence and prevalence);
   - infectious disease, including waterborne and tick-borne;
   - health inequality with figure reading;
   - Health in All Policies as policy history;
   - the ethical-viewpoints framework;
   - self-care and services.

## Recommendation [critic]

A TE2 exam drive is worth doing, narrower than TE1's: add to the existing pages, don't rewrite them, with at most one
new exam task per topic. In order of exam weight:
1. **te2-02:** research vocabulary, a risk-concept section with a three-text task, and a two-series chart task.
2. **te2-04:** radon facts with a task on a described risk map, an indoor-air source-and-effect table, water safety,
   and nature as a resource and a risk.
3. **te2-09 and te2-10:** substance harms in words, with no amounts, methods or figures (the pages stay flagged); the
   brain and the fetus; carcinogen as a concept; alcohol policy levers.
4. **te2-08:** working-life change and the work-ability house.
5. **Small items** in te2-01, te2-03, te2-05 and te2-06, plus a 20-item true/false set and a list-and-count task
   across the module.
