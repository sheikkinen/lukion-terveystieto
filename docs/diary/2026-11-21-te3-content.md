# Brief 010: TE3 content, accepted with minors after two fix rounds

**Date:** 2026-11-21 **IDs:** brief 010, D-007

## What happened

Six researchers and a critic set the ten TE3 topics (order 21–30), the link inventory, the TE3 safety rules (R1–R13:
law first, symmetric positions, no concluding for the student, no blame on disease pages) and 38 exam rows. The
content workflow wrote the topics; every TE3 code has a topic and an exam task. The re-mapper put full coverage of
TE3-touching exam points at 66.7% (partial 30.9%, none 2.4%), above the brief's 60%, but not "none at 0%". The judge
rejected the first round over 14 items (unsourced sentences, task-step caps, a cloze that rewarded guessing, missing
examples). A second fix round closed all 14 and added sourced entries on memory disorders, metabolic syndrome,
osteoporosis, functional capacity, salmonella, malaria and flu versus cold. The judge accepted with minors, on the
condition that the organ-donation fact box point at the current law; the producer fixed that and four minors.

## Traps

- **The judge can be wrong on a source.** Three checkers found the type 2 diabetes trend on the page the judge said
  lacked it. Contested sources need a checker who opens the page, not a ruling from memory.
- **Word counts include link URLs.** Three topics hit the 600-word Opiskelijalle limit, so new facts cite by name in
  the text and leave full links to the source list.
- **Data files lag the pages.** A fact box in `yearly-facts.yaml` contradicted the page above it after the page was
  fixed. Fix rounds must include the data owner.

## Heuristic

Name the coverage gaps in the page and the README rather than padding a topic to reach a number.

**Seed:** A small TE3 exam drive could close the named gaps (carcinogen classes, water treatment, child-mortality
measures, screening ages) once the line and word budgets are freed by splitting the busiest topics.
